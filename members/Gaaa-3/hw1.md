## 2. Pattern journal, Weeks 1 to 4

### Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role
I wrote three problem statements from things I had actually seen: a friend still paying for a subscription he had forgotten, a friend taking a 20–40% loan when a bank offered lower, and local businesses losing track of invoices. Reading them back, all three were shaped like apps rather than problems — I was pitching, not observing. I signed the team contract, including the clause that nobody may answer "that is not my role." 00-foundation/problem-pool.md

### Week 2 · Evidence over opinion
Nikoloz and I pooled six ideas into one file. Mine were the AI cash-flow, subscription and loans ideas. Reading each one out loud, the test was "did you see it, or did you imagine it?" I had seen all three happen, but none had a cost number attached, and nothing was tested that week. 00-foundation/problem-pool.md

### Week 3 · Kill your favorite idea
My subscription idea scored low on reach — I could not name five people I could interview about it in time. The wine idea died for the same reason even though it was the team's favourite. We moved to courses because five students were reachable immediately. The lesson was that reach kills ideas faster than anything else, whatever I think of them. DECISIONS.md

### Week 4 · The prototype is the conversation
I signed the team contract from my own account and we agreed the team name "Make Sense?". I did not run the prototype conversations that week — Nikoloz set up the repo, wrote the Georgian interview script and committed the problem hypothesis before our first interview. Honestly, most of Week 4 was Nikoloz's work, and I ran interviews 08 and 09 on the last night. 00-foundation/team-contract.md
