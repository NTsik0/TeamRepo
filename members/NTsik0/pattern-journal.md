## Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role

**Where I applied it:** Nobody assigned me a problem, so I went looking. I wrote three statements from things I had actually seen: a friend who needed C# before KIU offered it to him and took an outside course instead of waiting a semester; my friend Bachi and my parents in Batumi parking far from home and walking about 10 minutes every time; and village families who make their own wine while restaurants buy imported wine.

**Agency beyond my lane:** The parking statement did not come from guessing. For an earlier software homework I had asked people what their main problem was, and parking kept coming up, so I brought that evidence in rather than starting from an idea.

**What I hacked together:** I asked my parents and friends directly what annoys them day to day, instead of waiting for an interview script.

**Honest note:** my first versions were solutions ("an app that…"), not problems. I had to rewrite them as who / struggle / cost.

## Week 2 · Evidence over opinion

**Where I applied it:** We scored our shortlist with the Four Filters. My favorite idea, village wine, scored 3 of 8, because we could not name five farmers or restaurant owners to talk to (a 0 on reach). Courses scored 8 and became our pick. We kept wine as runner-up because we care about it, and wrote our dissent into the scorecard instead of hiding it.

**Agency beyond my lane:** I set up the team repo and the full semester folder structure, and moved our Lab 1 work into it.

**What I hacked together:** Instead of scoring "reach" from a feeling, I listed real names for each problem (Nini, Gio, Beka, Luka, Giorgi for courses; Bachi, my parents, Giorgi, Gaga for parking). The names decided the score.

**DECISIONS.md lines I drove:** line 1 (problem pick).
